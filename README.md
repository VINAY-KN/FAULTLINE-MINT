# FAULTLINE-MINT
FAULTLINE explores order and controlled disorder through generative geometry. Each hash shapes a field of shifting plates, varying in palette, spacing, relief, motifs, and layering, so every output feels both engineered and evolved, a different structural state of one system.

FAULTLINE is an exploration of structure, transformation, and controlled disorder through generative geometry.

I built FAULTLINE around a simple idea: what happens when a structured system is given enough freedom to continuously reorganize itself? I have always been drawn to transforming mechanical forms, futuristic structures, modular systems, and the feeling that a familiar object can become something entirely different through a change in its configuration. That fascination became the starting point for this project.

Each iteration is constructed from a field of geometric plates that can shift in scale, position, orientation, depth, color, and internal structure. Cellular patterns, layered surfaces, relief, embedded motifs, and intersecting forms create compositions that can feel architectural, geological, mechanical, or unfamiliar depending on the combination of elements. The work sits somewhere between something engineered and something that appears to have evolved on its own.

Color is an important part of that process. Earth, mineral, industrial, and atmospheric tones are combined with stronger chromatic accents, allowing the same underlying system to move between quieter compositions and more intense arrangements. I wanted the palettes to feel like they belong to the structure rather than simply being placed on top of it.

The system is deliberately constrained, but the relationships within those constraints are not fixed. The on-chain hash determines the decisions that shape each output, including the palette, composition, spacing, tiling, geometry, relief, orientation, internal motifs, and layering. Rather than designing individual images, I designed a set of rules and allowed each hash to produce its own structural state.

FAULTLINE is therefore less about a single visual formula and more about the space between order and disruption. Some iterations feel tightly assembled; others appear to separate, shift, or expand. Together they represent different states of the same generative system—variations that could not exist without the interaction between my decisions and the randomness provided by the algorithm.
